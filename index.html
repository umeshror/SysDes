Engineering Approach

How I Design Systems

Architecture is a series of tradeoffs, not a checklist. Every decision has a cost — in consistency, latency, operational complexity, or dollars. These are the principles that guide my work.

Design for the Failure Envelope, Not the Happy Path

CAP theorem is a starting point. Real constraints — device reboots, 72-hour offline periods, flaky mobile networks — determine the actual failure modes. I design for the real envelope, then validate assumptions with chaos testing.

Idempotency Is an Architectural Contract

In systems with reconnecting devices and unreliable transports, every operation will eventually be retried. Idempotency is not an afterthought — it's a first-class design requirement that determines whether retries are safe.

Observability Spans the Entire Stack

Monitoring tells you when something is wrong. Observability lets you debug failures you didn't anticipate. Edge-to-cloud distributed tracing across constrained devices and lossy transports requires deliberate instrumentation design — not just log aggregation.

Architectural Decisions Must Be Documented and Defensible

Every significant architectural choice should be traceable: what constraint drove it, what was traded away, and what would need to change for a different decision to be valid. I drive this through formal design proposals and reviews.
